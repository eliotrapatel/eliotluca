ORBES carries a line I wrote for it: "Permanent weight for those who curate their own existence." People ask what the second half means. It is the nearest thing I have to a philosophy of how to live, so I will try to explain it.

A curator does not make the works. A curator decides which ones enter the room, in what order, with how much space around each, and above all which ones stay out. The quality of an exhibition depends far more on what was refused than on what was hung. A room with everything in it is a warehouse.

I think a life works in the same way. Sartre argued that we have no nature given in advance, and that a person is only what they make of themselves through their choices. I find that broadly right, and I would add a practical detail: most of those choices are choices of admission. Which people I let close. Which objects I keep. Which habits I repeat. What I read, and what I allow to reach me every morning before I have had a thought of my own. A person is largely the sum of what they let in.

Most people do not curate. They accumulate. Things arrive by default: the acquaintances of circumstance, the opinions of whoever speaks loudest, the possessions bought to solve a passing mood. None of it was chosen, and after some years the result is a life that does not resemble anyone in particular.

Curating starts with refusal, which is why it is uncomfortable. Each thing I admit takes space that something better could have had, and my space is limited: so many hours, so much attention, so many people I can truly know. I try to treat every yes as expensive. I would rather have a few people I trust completely than many I know slightly, a few objects I will keep for decades than a house full of things I will replace.

This is where objects come in, and why I make them. An object that stays with you becomes a witness. It was there on a particular day, it marks a decision, it carries the years. Things that are bought and replaced cannot do this, because they are never around long enough. Choosing what to keep is a small daily exercise in the larger discipline.

I do not mean any of this as austerity. A well-curated room is full of things that belong together, with enough space left to see each of them. That is what I am trying to build with my days, and it is the person I had in mind when I wrote the line.
