I once wrote that I wanted to deserve my success. It is an easy sentence to write and a difficult one to defend, because of luck.

Nobody chooses where they are born, in which decade, with what health, to which family. Nobody chooses to be young at the moment a technology becomes available. A serious person has to admit that a large part of any result was decided before they made a single choice. So what can it mean to deserve anything?

I see two common answers, and I think both are wrong. The first is to ignore luck and credit everything to oneself. People who do this become arrogant, and worse, they stop understanding their own results, because they mistake good conditions for skill. The second is to credit everything to luck. It sounds modest, and it is often an excuse. If nothing is deserved, nothing is my responsibility, and there is no reason to try harder than I feel like trying.

My own position rests on an asymmetry. I do not know what share of my results comes from me. Nobody can know that about their own life. But the share that comes from me is the only one I can act on. So when I look at myself, I behave as if all of it were mine. If something fails, I search for my part in it and assume it was large. That assumption is probably false in some cases, and it is still the most useful one available, because it always leaves me with something to do.

When I look at other people, I reverse it. I assume luck played a large part in where they stand, for better or worse. I do not know what they were given or what they had to carry. It costs me nothing to be generous in that direction, and it keeps me from the contempt that successful people often develop for those who are behind them.

That leaves the word itself. I no longer think deserving is about the outcome. An outcome can be undeserved in either direction: a careless person can win and a careful one can lose. I think deserving is about the process. Did I do the work that would have produced this result in most versions of the world, and not only in the lucky one I happened to get? If so, the success is mine in the only sense that matters, and a failure would have been honourable.

This is a demanding standard, because it removes the comfort of results. A good year proves little. What I can ask each evening is narrower and harder: whether what I did today was the work of someone who would deserve it.
