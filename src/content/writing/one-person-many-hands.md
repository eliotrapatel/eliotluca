A few years ago, building what I am building would have required a team from the first day: engineers for the software, a designer for the interface, someone for the films, someone for the financial model. Today I do a large part of it myself, with AI tools doing much of the execution. I want to describe what this changes and what it does not, because I think both are widely misjudged.

What has changed is the cost of producing. I can describe a piece of software and have a working version the same day. I can draft, test and rebuild an interface several times in an afternoon. Work that used to mean hiring a specialist and waiting now means explaining clearly what I want and checking what comes back.

What has not changed is the need to know what good looks like. The tools produce whatever I ask for, including mediocre things, and they produce them fast. If I cannot tell a sound piece of code from a fragile one, or a balanced layout from a merely busy one, I will simply have more bad work than before. The bottleneck has moved from the hands to the judgement.

This is why I believe range matters more now than it did. I have spent years in design, in finance, in commerce and in software. None of that makes me the best specialist in any of them. It gives me a standard in each: I know when a model is wrong, when a page is off, when a price does not make sense. With AI doing the execution, that standard is the scarce part, and one person who holds it across several fields can direct work that used to need a department.

It has also changed how I spend my time. I write less and edit more. Most of my day is spent deciding what to make, saying precisely what I want, then rejecting or correcting the result. The skills that matter are the ones a good creative director or a good investor already has: a clear brief, a fast and honest review, the willingness to throw away something that took no effort to make.

There are things I keep entirely for myself. Choosing the problem. Setting the standard. Deciding what the product refuses to do. Those are the decisions that make a company what it is, and I have not found that they can be handed over.

I do not think this means companies will be one person for ever. It means the first version of almost anything can now be built by someone who understands the whole of it, and I find that a very good time to be a person who likes to understand the whole.
