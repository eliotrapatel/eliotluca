I practise eight disciplines at once, and I do it on purpose. Branding and design since 2016. Crypto since 2017. E-commerce from 2018 to 2021, and again from 2026. Finance since 2021. Music since 2023. AI since 2025. 3D modelling and watchmaking since 2026. I call it Da Vinci Mode.

The usual advice is to choose one field and go deep. It is good advice for a career inside an institution, where the reward goes to whoever is best at a narrow task that someone else defined. It is weaker advice for someone who wants to build. Building means taking decisions in several fields at the same time, and whoever can only see one of them has to hand the others to someone else.

I think of myself as a creator and an operator. The creator draws the logo, models the object and writes the product. The operator prices it, finances it, sells it and runs it. Most people are trained to be one of the two and to hire the other. I want to hold both, because the best decisions are taken by someone who can see the whole thing at once.

What I gain from working this way is that each field changes how I work in the others. Finance made me price everything: before committing to an idea I want to know what it costs, what else the money could do, and how much I can lose. Design gave me the opposite reflex, which is to remove, because a logo that tries to say four things says none of them. Writing software taught me that anything I do by hand twice should be done by a machine the third time. Making physical objects taught me that some mistakes cannot be corrected afterwards, which is a lesson software never gives you.

My two companies are what these habits produce together. THE ENTITY starts from a finance question, which companies gain when one part of the AI supply chain runs short. It answers that question with software, and it presents the answer with a designer's sense of what to show first. ORBES is a men's accessories brand, jewellery and leather goods, that I draw in three dimensions and sell online, and I think about its scarcity the way I learned to think about markets. I could not have built either one from inside a single field.

Leonardo is the model for this. His notebooks put hydraulics, anatomy and geometry on the same sheet, because to him they were one inquiry into how things move, hold and fit together. In Milan he worked alongside Luca Pacioli, the mathematician who first set out double-entry bookkeeping in print, in his *Summa* of 1494. Leonardo then drew the solids for Pacioli's *De divina proportione*, printed in 1509. A painter drew the figures for the man who put the ledger into print. Art and accounts sat at the same table, and that is the table I want to work at.

I do not intend to stop at eight. Each field I add gives the others something they lacked, and that is reason enough to keep adding.
