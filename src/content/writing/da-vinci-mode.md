I practise several disciplines at once, and I do it on purpose.

Branding since 2016. Crypto since 2017. E-commerce since 2018. Finance since 2021. Music since 2023. AI infrastructure since 2025. 3D modelling and watchmaking since 2026. Each date marks the point where I stopped reading about a field and started producing in it. I call the practice Da Vinci Mode. The name sets a standard to work towards.

The usual advice is to pick one lane and go deep. It is good advice for a career inside an institution, where the reward goes to whoever is best at a narrow task someone else defined. It is weaker advice for anyone who wants to build. Building means making decisions in several fields at the same time, and the person who can only see one of them hands the others to someone else, usually at a price.

Range has its own price, and that price is credibility. A list of interests is worth nothing. Anyone can write "designer, investor, musician" in a bio. Range becomes worth something only through the artefact: a mark a company actually uses, a model a decision was taken on, a deal document that went to buyers, a product that is live, a track that is finished, an object you can hold. If a discipline has not produced one, I have not earned the right to claim it.

The crossovers are the point. Each discipline trains a reflex the others lack.

Finance teaches you to price. Every choice carries a cost of capital, an opportunity cost and a downside you can size before you commit. Design teaches you to choose. A mark that tries to say four things says none, and most of the work is deciding what to remove. Code teaches you to automate. Anything you do by hand twice is a process waiting to be written once. Objects teach you tolerance. A setting that is a few tenths of a millimetre out will not hold its stone, and no amount of storytelling fixes it.

Put together, they correct each other. Pricing keeps design from becoming indulgent. Design keeps a financial model from becoming a spreadsheet nobody can read. Automation keeps a small team from drowning in manual work. Tolerance cures software people of the belief that everything can be patched after release. THE ENTITY, a map of the AI supply chain, is a finance question answered with software and laid out with a designer's hierarchy. ORBES is an object business run with an analyst's attention to scarcity and proof. Neither would exist if I had stayed in one lane.

Leonardo is the obvious reference, and the part worth copying is the method. His notebooks put hydraulics, anatomy and geometry on the same sheet, because for him they were one inquiry into how things move, hold and fit together. In Milan he worked alongside Luca Pacioli, the mathematician who first set out double-entry bookkeeping in print, in his *Summa* of 1494. Leonardo drew the solids for Pacioli's *De divina proportione*, printed in 1509. A painter drew the figures for the man who put the ledger into print. I take the method and leave the comparison.

The risk is real, and it has a name: dilettantism. The dilettante samples. He collects beginnings, speaks fluently about fields he has never shipped in, and mistakes curiosity for competence. Range without output is a more sophisticated way of avoiding judgement.

The rule against it is simple. Ship something real in each discipline, something a stranger could inspect and judge without my commentary. A mark in use. A model that held up. A product people can open. A piece that is finished. When a discipline stops producing, it comes off the list, whatever year I started it.

So, the credo.

Learn the fundamentals properly, then use them.

Produce the artefact before you claim the title.

Let each field audit the others.

Date every entry, so the record does the arguing.

Ship in all of them, or cut one.
