I first wrote this two years ago, in French, on a single page titled *Vision*. The idea has not moved. This is the same page, in English, with two more years behind it.

I want to be the sole author of my progress. My progress, my capital and the way that capital is put to work should answer to one person. I decide where the hours go, where the money goes and what is worth learning next. When a result is good, I want to know which decision of mine produced it. When it is bad, I want exactly the same.

That leads to the part most people avoid. If I fall short of what I set out to do, there is one person to blame, and I choose it that way. Blame shared with a market, a partner or bad timing is comfortable, and it teaches nothing, because none of it is mine to change. Blame that stops at my own door is expensive, and it is the only kind I can act on. Taking all of it is how I keep all of the control.

Control starts inside. Events will do what they do: prices move, people change their minds, plans meet the world. None of that is mine to command. What is mine is what those events are allowed to reach: my judgement, my emotions, my motivation, my capital. I work so that no outside force has a direct line to any of them. A bad day in a market can cost me money I chose to put at risk. It does not get to decide how I think the next morning.

The same rule holds on the way down. If my success has to be earned by me, my failure has to be mine as well. So I arrange my affairs to leave no one else the power to ruin me: no single counterparty, no single dependency, no one whose approval I cannot do without. I would rather own a smaller thing entirely than a larger thing on someone else's terms.

All of it rests on one skill, which is knowing myself with precision. I treat my own mind as an instrument with settings. How much risk I carry well. How I decide when I am tired. What restores my energy and what drains it. Which kind of praise makes me careless. The work is to learn those settings the way an expert learns a field, then adjust them deliberately, the way an operator tunes a machine he built himself. That is what self-mastery means to me: expertise, applied to oneself.

The original page ended on a plain sentence, and I keep it. This is how I reach my full success: sole author of the progress, sole owner of the outcome, and a lifelong student of the one variable present in every decision I will ever make.
