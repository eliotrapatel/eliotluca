"We sleep when we die" is one of the lines ORBES carries. It is usually read as bravado, the boast of someone who works all night. I sleep perfectly well, and I recommend it. The line is about something else.

It is about the fact that time is the only thing I have that cannot be replaced. Money can be lost and made again. A company can fail and another be built. A reputation can be repaired. An hour, once spent, is gone on the same terms for everyone, and nobody knows how many they have.

Seneca wrote about this in a short text on the shortness of life. His argument is that life is not short at all. It is long enough for a great deal, and we make it short by wasting it. He observes that people guard their money and their property closely, and then hand out their time to anyone who asks, as if it cost nothing. I have read few things that describe ordinary life more exactly.

I try to hold both halves of that. The first half is urgency. If the time is limited and the amount unknown, then what I want to build should be started now, and waiting for better conditions is mostly a way of spending years. I see many people preparing for a life they intend to begin later. I would rather begin badly today.

The second half is selectiveness, and it is the more important one. Urgency without selection is only haste. Knowing that hours are finite does not mean filling each of them with activity. It means deciding which things deserve them and refusing the rest without guilt. A day given to something I chose, done with full attention, is a day well spent whether I worked four hours or fourteen. A day lost to things I drifted into is lost however busy it was.

There is a calm in this that surprised me. I expected that thinking often about the limit would make me anxious. It has done the reverse. Many things that used to weigh on me, such as what people think or whether a small plan succeeds, do not survive the question of whether they will matter at the end. Most of them do not, and they lose their grip.

So the line is closer to a reminder than a boast. Rest will come in the end without any effort on my part. What will not come by itself is the work I am here to do, and the time for that is now, while there is some.
