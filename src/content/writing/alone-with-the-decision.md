Anyone who wants to be the author of their own life has to accept a consequence that is rarely mentioned: at the moment of deciding, you are alone.

Advice is plentiful. I can ask ten people what they would do, and most will answer gladly. But advice and responsibility are different things. The person who advises me goes home afterwards. If the decision is wrong, the cost is mine, and so the decision has to be mine too. However many voices I gather beforehand, there comes a point where they stop and one person chooses.

Many people find that moment so uncomfortable that they arrange never to face it. They decide by committee, or follow what everyone around them is doing, or wait until circumstances have decided for them. Each of these spreads the responsibility until nobody holds it. It feels safer. The price is that such a person never learns what they themselves think.

Pascal wrote that all of man's unhappiness comes from one thing, which is not knowing how to stay quietly in a room. I think he was describing the same weakness. A person who cannot be alone with his thoughts cannot be alone with a question either, and will hand it to someone else as fast as he can.

So I have tried to train this deliberately. For any decision that matters, I collect what others know, and I listen properly, because they often see what I cannot. Then I take the question away with me, and I stay with it until I have an answer I can defend without quoting anyone. The test is simple. If I am asked later why I chose this, and my answer begins with someone else's name, I have not decided. I have obeyed.

There is a kind of solitude in this that I have learned to value. It is not loneliness. I am not short of people, and I do not think anyone builds much entirely alone. It is the quiet of knowing that what I do next comes from my own judgement, for better or worse. When it goes well, I know why. When it goes badly, I know where to look. Both are worth more to me than the comfort of having shared the blame.

It does have a price, and I would be dishonest to leave it out. There are evenings when a decision is heavy and there is nobody whose job it is to carry it with me. I have come to think that this weight is what authorship is. Each time I take it up without looking for someone to hand it to, I am a little more capable of carrying the next one.

This is also why I choose the few people close to me with such care. They cannot decide for me. But they are the ones I return to after the room, and their quality matters more than their number.
